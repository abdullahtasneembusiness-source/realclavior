At twelve-ten, the two climbers reached the summit in a whiteout. Visibility was down to a few metres, and the wind was rising off the ridge. They had a plan for this: descend the north-west ridge, a straightforward walk-off in clear weather. But with no visual reference, and a compass they never re-checked, they turned downhill onto the fall line — the steepest, most direct way down, and exactly the wrong one. It was the last decision they got to make freely.
